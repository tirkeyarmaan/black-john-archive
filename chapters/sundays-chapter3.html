Somewhere in Korba, Chhattisgarh.

December has a strange way of making ordinary days feel like they're part of something bigger. Maybe it's the weather, maybe it's the Christmas decorations, or maybe it's just the fact that everyone suddenly remembers how to be cheerful.

For me, December began with flowers.

FLOWERBOY

It was around the first or second Sunday of December. After Sunday school, Aanya and I were meeting up with Arman and Ishaan. A rather corny little couples' gathering, if you ask me.

Everyone was having fun when Arman suddenly came up with an idea.

He told me to pluck some flowers from a nearby tree and give them to Aanya. Naturally, he would record the entire thing.

Arman had a blueprint. That was about it. No proper script, no rehearsals, no professional equipment. Just a phone camera and a group of people willing to participate in whatever nonsense he had planned.

I was standing beside a tree covered in pink and purple flowers when one little flower fell right onto my head.

Everyone noticed.

And just like that, the music video began.

What followed was a completely improvised flower-giving scene. Arman was recording, everyone was having fun, and I was somehow the main character of a production I hadn't auditioned for.

But the real plot twist was yet to come.

Aanya's mother suddenly spotted her standing alongside me.

I swear, my brain stopped functioning. I was terrified. One moment I was participating in a flower video, and the next I felt like I'd been caught doing something terribly illegal.

We eventually said our goodbyes, but the incident stayed in my head for the rest of the day.

In the evening, I messaged Aanya and asked what had happened after that.

Apparently, her mother had simply asked her my name and made a comment about tribal boys being brown.

I won't pretend that comment didn't feel uncomfortable. It did.

But somehow, everything went smoothly.

And then came the nickname.

Flowerboy.

Her mother gave me that name, and Aanya started using it too. Honestly, I kinda liked it. It had a nice ring to it.

Tyler, the Creator had an entire album. I had a nickname given to me by someone's mother.

Not a bad achievement for a Sunday afternoon.

THE NATIVITY

The Christmas gathering came after the Flowerboy incident.

Our colony had a performance planned for the event. The adults would sing a song about the birth of Christ while we acted out the events without using any dialogues.

A silent performance. No speaking, just acting.

And somehow, I ended up playing Joseph.

There wasn't much competition for the role. Someone simply asked if I could play him, and I agreed.

That was my entire audition.

Mary, on the other hand, was played by someone from another area. I had known her for a long time, though she was older than me. Her family was quite well known, so somehow she ended up being part of our performance.

For baby Jesus, we initially used a doll during the preparations.

But on the actual event day, Mary held a real baby.

And so, there we were: Joseph, Mary, and a real baby, performing the story of the birth of Christ while the adults sang in the background.

The performance itself was one thing.

What happened afterwards was another.

AN UNPLANNED FAMILY

After the performance, my friends decided that the show wasn't over.

They started joking that I was having a baby with another woman.

Apparently, my family had already arranged this marriage for me.

I don't know how they managed to turn a religious performance into a marriage announcement, but they did.

It became a whole joyride for them.

Unfortunately, Aanya wasn't enjoying it.

The jokes kept going, and eventually she started crying.

That was when the atmosphere changed for me.

Something that had seemed funny to everyone else had genuinely hurt someone. I hadn't thought much about it while everyone was laughing, but seeing her upset made me feel guilty.

I tried to talk to her and find a place where we could speak properly.

And, because apparently December wasn't done surprising me, I accidentally cut my hand while arranging things.

It wasn't serious, but there was a little blood.

Aanya noticed it immediately.

She took out her handkerchief and wrapped it around my hand.

I was trying to find the words to apologise for hurting her feelings, and she was busy taking care of my hand.

I told her I was sorry. I hadn't realised that my silly behaviour and the jokes could mean so much to someone.

I never intended to hurt her, but I understood that not intending to do something didn't automatically make it okay.

Eventually, things returned to normal between us.

And just like that, the incident came to an end.

THE THINGS WE LEARN WITHOUT REALISING

Looking back, December gave me two rather different memories.

One involved a flower falling on my head, an improvised music video, and a nickname that I secretly enjoyed.

The other involved a Christmas performance, a few jokes that went too far, and a handkerchief wrapped around my hand.

I suppose growing up isn't always about grand revelations or life-changing speeches.

Sometimes it's about noticing when a joke stops being funny.

Sometimes it's about apologising.

And sometimes, it's about remembering that the people around us have feelings that we might not always understand immediately.

I had started December as Flowerboy.

I ended up learning something a little more important than how to pose for a camera.

End of Chapter 3.
