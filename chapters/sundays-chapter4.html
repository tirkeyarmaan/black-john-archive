Somewhere in Korba, Chhattisgarh.

December left quietly.

No dramatic goodbye. No final scene with background music. Christmas decorations slowly disappeared, people returned to their routines, and life went back to doing what it does best: happening without asking for permission.

January arrived. Then February.

Two months.

It's strange how you can spend so much time waiting for something interesting to happen, only to realise later that those ordinary days were the story all along.

THE USUAL ROUTINE

Sundays continued.

The same church, the same familiar faces, the same Sunday-school routine. Adults attended the first Mass while we had our classes, and later everyone gathered again for the second Mass.

Nothing particularly extraordinary.

Friends still found reasons to laugh at absolutely nothing. Someone always had something to complain about, someone always had a story to tell, and someone was probably doing something they shouldn't have been doing.

In other words, everything was normal.

School continued too. Assignments, classes, unfinished work, and the occasional moment when I wondered whether education was a carefully designed experiment to test human patience.

I was still me. Still writing, still sketching, still collecting random thoughts that probably made more sense on paper than they ever would out loud.

THE LAST CLASS

January brought something else with it.

I completed Class 12 in Sunday school.

That was it. Twelve classes, years of lessons, activities, familiar faces and Sunday mornings. I'd officially completed the programme. There was nothing left to continue in Sunday school.

It felt strange.

For years, attending Sunday school had simply been part of my routine. You wake up, get ready, go to church, attend class, meet your friends and eventually go home.

You don't really think about how long you've been doing it until suddenly, there's no next class to attend.

Of course, I wasn't leaving the church. I'd still come for Mass, still meet familiar faces, and still spend time with my friends.

But something had changed.

I was no longer one of the Sunday school students.

It was a small milestone, perhaps, but it marked the end of something that had been part of my life for years.

And just like that, another chapter closed without making too much noise.

THE QUIETER DAYS

Things between Aanya and me had become quieter.

We weren't exactly distant. There wasn't some big argument or dramatic disagreement. We just weren't talking the way we used to.

Sometimes, nothing happens between two people. No argument, no dramatic goodbye. Just silence filling spaces that conversations once occupied.

And honestly, I didn't think much of it back then.

Life had its own rhythm. There were friends to meet, things to do, and plenty of distractions.

Not every silence feels like a warning. Sometimes it just feels like a normal Tuesday.

TWO MONTHS LATER

January passed without asking for permission.

February followed, bringing more ordinary Sundays, familiar conversations, and days that seemed almost identical to the ones before them.

Looking back, I don't think those months were empty.

They were simply quiet.

And perhaps that's why I remember them differently now. When you're living through ordinary days, you rarely realise you're collecting memories.

Then came March.

And apparently, March had other plans.
