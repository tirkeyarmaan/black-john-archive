THE UNEXPECTED QUESTION

I couldn't believe my eyes.

I kept looking at the letter as if it were some kind of secret document from the government.

"Maybe I like you, will be my bf."

With hearts.

Lots of hearts.

I don't know what I expected to happen after receiving a letter like that, but asking for a romantic confession certainly wasn't on my bingo card.

I had no immediate answer.

So I did something unusual.

I asked Anushka to tell her that I needed some time to think.

Yes.

A boy had received a confession and was asking for time to respond.

I know. Hilarious.

Somewhere, Shakespeare was probably reconsidering his career.

THE FRIENDS' COURTROOM

I eventually discussed the whole situation with my friends.

And by discussed, I mean I brought the matter before a group of people who were absolutely not qualified to give relationship advice.

Me: "So, there's this girl..."

Friend: "OHHHH!"

Me: "Can you let me finish?"

Friend: "Continue, Romeo."

I explained everything. Anushka, the introduction, Aanya and the letter.

Everyone had something to say.

But Ishaan wasn't reacting quite like the others.

He was a very good friend of Angel, and he knew something that made the situation more complicated.

Angel liked me too.

I hadn't really considered that.

Ishaan eventually asked me something.

Ishaan: "So, what's your decision?"

Me: "I don't know yet."

Ishaan: "Think about it properly. Your decision could make someone's day and break someone's heart at the same time."

That was a rather serious statement coming from someone who could otherwise be responsible for half the nonsense happening around us.

I understood what he meant.

There were two people involved, and my decision could affect both of them.

But I also knew that I couldn't choose someone simply because I felt guilty about hurting someone else.

After thinking about it, I decided that I wanted to give things a chance with Aanya.

I wasn't in love. I wasn't suddenly a romantic hero from a Bollywood film.

I was simply interested in her and wanted to know her better.

Angel was disappointed.

She was sad, naturally. But she understood that it was my decision. She didn't turn it into some dramatic rivalry or blame anyone.

Life continued.

At least, that's what I thought.

THE BREAKFAST REVELATION

Some time passed.

One morning, I was having breakfast with my friends at a café.

It was a pretty ordinary morning. We were eating, talking and discussing whatever random things came to mind.

Then Francis casually brought something up.

Francis: "You know she's very young compared to you, right?"

I didn't immediately understand what he meant.

Me: "Who?"

Francis: "Aanya."

Me: "What about her?"

Francis: "She's much younger than you. She lives in Police Line, doesn't she?"

I nodded.

Then he told me her age.

I was seventeen.

She was thirteen.

Four years.

...

WHAT?

I looked at Francis.

Me: "Are you being serious?"

Francis: "Yes, I'm serious."

I genuinely couldn't process it.

How had I not thought about this earlier?

I knew she was younger, but I hadn't properly considered the difference.

It was like discovering that the game you'd been playing for hours had an entirely different set of rules.

Except this wasn't a video game.

It was real life.

And I had been rather silly about something important.

For once, I couldn't just laugh it off.

THE QUESTION OUTSIDE ANUSHKA'S HOUSE

We usually met on Sundays.

When I saw Aanya again, I decided to ask her directly.

The conversation happened outside Anushka's house, somewhere between evening and night.

I looked at her.

Me: "Do ya know how old am I?"

Aanya: "Yes, I know."

Me: "You still made this decision?"

Aanya: "Yes. Does it still matter?"

I was caught off guard by her confidence.

I didn't know what to say for a moment.

Me: "Awwww."

I couldn't help reacting that way.

But looking back, there was something I hadn't understood properly at the time.

At seventeen and thirteen, four years isn't just a number. It's a significant difference in where two people are in life.

Her feelings could be genuine, but I still had a responsibility to recognise that difference.

I didn't handle that situation as thoughtfully as I should have.

Sometimes growing up means looking back at your younger self and realising that you had a lot left to learn.

THE COLONY ROAD INCIDENT

That evening, something else happened.

Aanya kissed me on the cheek.

I was completely caught off guard.

I don't know how to describe that particular moment without sounding like a character from some excessively dramatic romance film.

But I remember thinking:

It felt like the touch of God.

Yes, I know. Very dramatic.

If someone had put a slow-motion filter over the scene, I probably would've looked like the protagonist of a low-budget romantic film.

I kept the incident to myself.

Or at least, I thought I had.

From a distance, some of Aanya's friends had seen what happened.

They couldn't make out every detail, but they knew something had happened.

And because it took place right in the middle of the colony road, it became a running joke among the girls.

Apparently, privacy was a luxury I hadn't subscribed to.

I didn't know about their little joke at the time.

LETTERS, LIKE THE OLD DAYS

One thing I always liked was writing.

I could spend hours sketching something, writing random ideas for comics, or coming up with ridiculous conversations between fictional characters.

And somehow, that habit found its way into my everyday life.

Letters.

Handwritten letters.

In a world where everyone had a phone, I still preferred writing things down.

I suppose I was old-school.

Or perhaps I had watched too many films where people wrote letters and thought it was a pretty cool thing to do.

I usually initiated the exchanges.

They weren't long letters. Nothing like the dramatic declarations you'd find in an old romantic novel.

They were short notes.

A little joke here.

Something about her there.

A random thought.

An incident from the day.

Sometimes I'd write something philosophical, only to ruin the entire mood with an unnecessary joke.

That was just how I was.

I could go from discussing the meaning of life to making a reference nobody understood within approximately five seconds.

The letters became a small part of our routine.

And I liked writing them.

It was easier to put certain thoughts on paper than to say everything out loud.

Looking back, I think that period taught me quite a few things about myself.

I was inexperienced, sometimes careless, occasionally ridiculous and often convinced that I understood more about life than I actually did.

I had a lot to learn about feelings, responsibility and the consequences of my decisions.

But life doesn't pause while you're figuring yourself out.

The Sundays kept coming.

The church remained the centre of our little world.

My friends continued being themselves.

And before long, the atmosphere around us began to change.

The year was approaching one of its most wonderful seasons.

Christmas was coming.

And if there's one thing I can say about Christmas in church, it's that things rarely stay quiet for very long.

End of Chapter 2.
