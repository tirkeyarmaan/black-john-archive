THE HOUSE DOESN'T SLEEP · CHAPTER ONE

The Last Road

BELLWETHER COUNTY · NOVEMBER 1996

8:17 PM

The radio had been playing the same song for almost twenty minutes.

Not because the driver particularly liked it. He had simply forgotten to change the station.

Outside, November rain crawled down the windshield in thin, crooked lines. The headlights illuminated a narrow stretch of asphalt, surrounded by pine trees that seemed to grow directly out of the darkness.

The road was empty.

It had been empty for the last forty miles.

A cigarette burned between the driver's fingers.

He glanced at the dashboard clock.

8:17.

He looked back at the road.

8:17.

"Wonderful."

He tapped the clock twice. It continued displaying the same time.

The radio crackled.

For a moment, the music slowed down, as though somebody were turning an invisible dial.

Then a man's voice interrupted it.

"...remain indoors until further notice."

The driver frowned.

"What?"

Static.

"...Bellwether residents are advised..."

The transmission disappeared.

He reached for the radio controls.

A burst of music returned.

He turned it off.

The silence was immediate.

A few seconds passed.

Then something struck the underside of the car.

Thump.

He gripped the steering wheel.

Another sound.

Thump.

He slowed down and glanced into the rear-view mirror.

Nothing.

"Bloody roads."

He continued driving.

1. The Cabin

The Hollow Cabin wasn't particularly impressive.

It was an old wooden building standing several metres away from the road, surrounded by trees and an overgrown patch of grass. A rusted mailbox leaned beside the entrance. Someone had left a broken garden chair underneath the porch.

There were no lights outside.

The driver parked his car.

He sat for a moment, watching the cabin.

A yellow light glowed behind one of the windows.

He checked the address written on a folded piece of paper.

Then he looked at the building again.

"Well, that's something."

He stepped outside.

The rain had become lighter, although the air was cold enough to make him regret leaving his jacket in the passenger seat.

He approached the porch and knocked.

Nothing.

He knocked again.

From somewhere inside came the sound of a chair scraping against the floor.

The door opened.

A woman stood on the other side.

She appeared to be in her late twenties. Her dark hair was tied loosely behind her head, and she wore a grey sweater with sleeves rolled up to her elbows.

She looked at him.

"You're late."

The man checked his watch.

"Am I?"

She stared at him for another second.

Then she stepped aside.

"Come in."

He entered.

There were already four other people inside.

A broad-shouldered man sat near the fireplace, cleaning his glasses with the corner of his shirt. A young woman occupied the sofa, reading a magazine. Another man stood beside the kitchen counter, attempting to open a bottle with a house key.

The fifth person was nowhere to be seen.

"Well," said the man with the glasses, "another one."

"Another what?" asked the newcomer.

"Person."

The woman with the magazine turned a page.

"He's been doing that all evening."

"Doing what?"

"Being annoying."

The man with the glasses put his spectacles back on.

"Frankly, I think I'm being very welcoming."

The newcomer placed his suitcase near the wall.

"Is this some kind of meeting?"

Nobody answered immediately.

The woman in the grey sweater closed the door.

"No. We're all just waiting for the weather to clear."

"Waiting for what?"

"Morning."

He nodded slowly.

"Right."

A brief silence followed.

The man beside the counter finally opened his bottle.

"Anybody want one?"

"No," said the woman on the sofa.

"Didn't ask you."

She lowered her magazine.

"Then stop looking at me."

The newcomer sighed.

He was beginning to suspect that this would be a very long evening.

2. Six People

By nine o'clock, everyone had introduced themselves, although not particularly enthusiastically.

The woman in the grey sweater was Helen.

The man with the glasses was Arthur.

The woman reading the magazine was Claire.

The man beside the kitchen counter was Mason.

The newcomer with the suitcase was Daniel.

And the sixth person, who had apparently been upstairs when everyone arrived, was Elliot.

Elliot eventually appeared carrying a blanket and a half-empty box of cereal.

"Is there milk?"

Nobody responded.

He looked around.

"Fantastic."

He went into the kitchen.

Daniel sat beside the fireplace.

"So, how did everyone end up here?"

"Car trouble," Mason answered.

"Same," said Arthur.

Claire raised her hand slightly.

"Wrong turn."

Helen shrugged.

"Needed somewhere to stay."

Daniel looked towards Elliot.

"And you?"

Elliot was eating cereal directly from the box.

"Long story."

"Fair enough."

"Actually, no. Not really."

He returned to eating.

The conversation eventually shifted towards the weather, the road conditions and whether the cabin had enough blankets.

It was ordinary.

Comfortably ordinary.

Outside, the rain continued.

3. The Upstairs Room

At approximately 9:36 PM, Claire went looking for a bathroom.

She found one at the end of the upstairs corridor.

The corridor was narrow, with faded wallpaper and a single ceiling bulb that occasionally flickered.

She returned several minutes later.

"There's a room upstairs."

Helen looked up from the table.

"Yes?"

"Why is it locked?"

"Which room?"

"At the end of the hallway."

Helen thought for a moment.

"Probably storage."

"There's somebody knocking inside."

The room became quiet.

Mason stopped pouring his drink.

Arthur looked at her.

"Knocking?"

Claire nodded.

"Three times."

Elliot leaned back in his chair.

"Maybe the storage has developed a personality."

Nobody laughed.

Helen stood up.

"I'll check."

They followed her upstairs.

The corridor was empty.

The locked door stood at the end, painted a dull shade of brown. Its brass handle was tarnished, and a small number had been nailed above it.

The number was 7.

Helen tried the handle.

Locked.

She knocked.

Nothing.

She waited.

Then knocked again.

Nothing.

"See?" she said.

Claire crossed her arms.

"I heard it."

"Probably the pipes."

Helen started walking back downstairs.

Arthur remained behind for a moment.

He looked at the door.

There was something strange about the number.

It appeared freshly painted.

He reached towards it.

The wood around the nail was old and dark.

The number itself was white.

Arthur returned downstairs without mentioning it.

4. 10:12 PM

10:12 PM

The electricity went out.

The entire cabin fell into darkness.

Somebody swore.

A chair scraped against the floor.

"Brilliant," Elliot muttered.

Mason found a box of matches in the kitchen drawer. Helen lit three candles and placed them on the table.

Outside, the rain had stopped.

That was the first thing Daniel noticed.

The second was the absence of wind.

The forest beyond the windows was completely still.

Not quiet.

Still.

Like a photograph.

"Does anyone have a flashlight?" Arthur asked.

"I've got one in the car," Daniel replied.

"I'll come with you."

They stepped onto the porch.

The air smelled of wet wood and soil.

Daniel unlocked his car and retrieved a flashlight from the glove compartment.

Arthur stood beside him, looking towards the trees.

"Strange place."

"You're telling me."

Arthur pointed towards the road.

"Did you see that?"

Daniel turned.

"What?"

"Nothing."

They stood there for another moment.

Then Arthur looked towards the cabin.

"Let's go inside."

5. Something in the Trees

Inside, the others had begun arguing about the electricity.

Mason insisted it was probably a local outage.

Elliot suggested that perhaps the cabin was haunted.

Claire told him to shut up.

Helen was searching through a cupboard for spare candles.

Then Daniel noticed something.

A window.

It was slightly open.

He approached it and looked outside.

The trees were black against the cloudy sky.

He closed the window.

For a moment, his reflection stared back at him.

Behind it, somewhere beyond the glass, something moved.

Daniel turned.

Nothing.

He looked outside again.

Nothing.

"Everything alright?" Helen asked.

"Yeah."

He stepped away from the window.

The conversation resumed.

The candles burned lower.

And the clock above the fireplace continued displaying 10:12.

6. The Last Road

10:47 PM

Somewhere beyond the cabin, deep inside the forest, something was moving.

The road was empty.

The trees stood motionless.

There was no rain.

No wind.

No distant traffic.

Only darkness.

And then, between two pine trees, a figure appeared.

It stood upright.

Its movements were slow and deliberate.

It took one step.

Then another.

A human silhouette.

The figure moved closer.

Its shoulders shifted.

Its head tilted slightly to one side.

Another step.

The perspective lowered.

The movement became uneven.

Something brushed against the wet leaves.

A shape crossed the path.

Four limbs.

A narrow back.

Something resembling an animal.

But the silhouette didn't quite belong to any animal.

It stopped.

For several seconds, it remained completely motionless.

Then its head turned towards the cabin.

The cabin was visible now.

One yellow light glowed through the curtains.

The figure moved again.

Closer.

Closer.

The perspective began to distort.

The trees appeared unusually tall. The ground seemed to rise and fall beneath it. The movement no longer resembled walking, crawling or running.

It was simply getting closer.

Inside the cabin, someone laughed.

A chair scraped.

A voice said something about the electricity.

The figure reached the edge of the clearing.

The porch was empty.

The front door was closed.

Through the window, six people could be seen sitting around a table.

The perspective moved towards the porch.

A step.

Another.

Then—

The porch light switched off.

Inside, the laughter stopped.

"Did somebody touch the switch?" Mason asked.

Nobody answered.

A sound came from outside.

Three slow knocks.

Knock.

Knock.

Knock.

Everyone looked towards the front door.

Helen stood up.

"Hello?"

Silence.

She approached the door.

"Who's there?"

Nothing.

Then, from somewhere directly above her—

A fourth knock.

Knock.

Helen looked towards the ceiling.

The others followed her gaze.

And the camera—

The perspective—

Whatever it was—

Remained outside.

Looking at the cabin.

Waiting.

END OF CHAPTER ONE.
